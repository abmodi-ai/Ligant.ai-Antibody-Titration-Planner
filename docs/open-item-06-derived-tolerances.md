Open item 6 asks for the round-trip, unit-normalisation and ratio-test
tolerances to be derived as analytic bounds over the stated operation set, per
C4-CN-01 as amended at v0.5: **a tolerance listed as derived is the analytic
bound, and an empirical distribution is evidence that the bound is not loose,
never the tolerance itself.** The build's earlier ratio-test figure, 4 ULP, was
a sample maximum over 3.3 million cases and is withdrawn on exactly that
ground; it is kept below as the evidence it always was.

Sign-off is NADIRA's, on this record and not on a summary of it. The register
rows for all three tolerances stay OPEN until that review; what follows is the
record that review is against.

## The stated generation method, per C4-IV-06

Each point is computed from the top point and its integer index as
`top / f^(i-1)`, with `f^(i-1)` formed by **exponentiation by squaring** and
applied as a **single division**. `Math.pow` is not used. Points are not
generated by repeated multiplication from the preceding point.

All three tolerances below are derived over this method and the normalisation
path of `normalise.ts`, and no other. A change to either invalidates the
derivation, not merely the numbers.

## (a) The reference quantity for every ULP figure

Every figure below is measured by `ulpsBetween(a, b)`
(`src/lib/ulp.ts`): `|a - b|` divided by the spacing between adjacent
representable doubles **at the larger magnitude of the two values compared**.
This is a distance between two ACTUAL COMPUTED VALUES, not a distance from a
value to some idealised infinite-precision reference: "1 ULP" states that the
two values compared are adjacent representable doubles, the smallest possible
disagreement, and every figure in this document is that distance applied to a
specific, stated pair:

| Tolerance | What is compared |
|---|---|
| Round-trip (C4-IV-01) | the original swept concentration, against the same concentration after volume-and-back |
| Unit-normalisation (C4-IV-04) | `volumePerConcentration` computed via the base-unit path, against the same quantity computed via the non-base-unit path, for the same physical declaration |
| Ratio-test (C4-IV-03) | the computed consecutive ratio `points[i-1] / points[i]`, against the declared dilution factor `f` |

None of these is a relative-error percentage or an absolute microlitre
tolerance: all three are ULP counts, for the reason `ulp.ts` states, an
absolute tolerance means different things at different magnitudes and a
relative one is the same as this to within a factor of two while being harder
to reason about.

## 1. Round-trip tolerance: analytic bound 1 ULP, compared with "less than or equal"

C4-IV-01: a concentration converted to a volume per test and back, at the same
staining volume and stock concentration, returns the input.

### The derivation

The round trip is two operations, a multiply and a divide by the SAME folded
constant (`volumePerConcentration`), because `normalise.ts` folds the staining
volume and the stock concentration into one number at entry. Each operation
contributes at most half an ULP, so the analytic bound is **1 ULP**.

Applied stepwise the trip would be six operations and the bound three times as
loose. C1 measured precisely that on its own two-factor conversion and found
the stepwise path reaching 3.0 ULP and exceeding a 1 ULP bound in 0.54 per cent
of cases, against zero exceedances folded, and additionally found the stepwise
intermediate underflowing where the folded divisor did not. **A tool
inheriting this row as an observation would fail the bound.** The figure is
not transferable without the structure that produces it.

### (b) This measurement runs on ALREADY-NORMALISED values

**Yes.** The 200,000-case sweep below (`src/lib/invariance.test.ts`,
`sweep()`) generates `concentration`, `stockUgPerMl` and `stainingVolumeUl`
directly, as already-base-unit numbers, and computes `volumePerConcentration =
stainingVolumeUl / stockUgPerMl` from them without going through
`normalise()`'s unit-conversion step at all. It measures the round trip
ALONE, taking the folded constant as given. It says nothing about the
conversion that PRODUCES that constant from a value entered in mL or mg/mL,
which is a separate operation with its own rounding.

That gap is what C4-IV-04 is for, and why it now has its own register row
rather than inheriting this one: see section 3 below.

**Measured**: 200,000 cases, concentrations across eleven decades, stock
concentrations across eight, staining volumes across five. Worst observed
error **1.0 ULP**, zero cases exceeding. This distribution is evidence the
1 ULP analytic bound is exact, not loose, not the bound itself.

## (c) Which inputs were varied, and how

`sweep()`'s generator is a deterministic xorshift PRNG (`No Math.random: the
suite must not vary`, per its own comment), not a draw from a fixed table of
round numbers. Each of the three swept quantities is `10 ** (next() * span -
offset)`, i.e. a continuous, non-round real exponent over its stated decade
range:

| Quantity | Range | Distribution |
|---|---|---|
| Concentration | 1e-5 to 1e6 (eleven decades) | continuous, `10 ** (uniform(0,1) * 11 - 5)` |
| Stock concentration | 1e-2 to 1e6 (eight decades) | continuous, `10 ** (uniform(0,1) * 8 - 2)` |
| Staining volume | 1e-1 to 1e4 (five decades) | continuous, `10 ** (uniform(0,1) * 5 - 1)` |

Because the exponent itself is a continuous real number rather than an
integer, the overwhelming majority of the 200,000 drawn values are non-round
in every sense: not round decimals, not round binary fractions, not exact
powers of ten. A generator restricted to round values (exact decades, or
values with short binary expansions) would systematically avoid the roundings
this tolerance exists to bound, which is precisely the failure mode C4-FX-01
and C4-FX-06 are separately constructed to rule out for the fixture set.

## (d) Confirmed capable of failing, with each inserted defect's magnitude

C4-IV-02 requires three defects, a clamp, a floor and a nudge, each shown to
be DETECTED and to EXCEED the derived tolerance by a recorded margin.
Measured in `src/lib/invariance.test.ts`, on the six points of the reference
case (`acceptance 7, the round trip is confirmed capable of failing`):

| Defect | What it does | Points reached | Magnitude reached | Detected against 1 ULP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Clamp** | pins the round-trip volume at a 4 µL ceiling before dividing back | 1 of 6 (the point whose volume exceeds 4 µL) | **> 1e12 ULP** | Yes |
| **Floor** | lifts the round-trip volume to a 2 µL floor before dividing back | 4 of 6 (the points whose volume is below 2 µL) | **> 1e12 ULP** | Yes |
| **Nudge, 2 ULP** | displaces the intermediate volume by exactly 2 units in the last place | 6 of 6 (every point; not a range defect) | exactly **2.0 ULP** on every point | Yes |

The clamp and the floor are RANGE defects: each reaches only the points on its
own side of its threshold, and the exact counts (1 and 4, on the reference
case) are asserted rather than "at least one", so that a future change moving
a point across either threshold shows up as a changed count rather than a
silently-passing test.

**Sensitivity, bounded on both sides**, per the same discipline C4-IV-05
requires of the ratio test:

| Inserted nudge | Measured | Detected against the 1 ULP bound |
|---|---|---|
| 1 ULP | exactly 1.0 ULP | **No.** Sits precisely on the bound |
| 2 ULP | exactly 2.0 ULP | Yes, on all six points |
| 4 ULP | exactly 4.0 ULP | Yes |

A displacement of exactly one ULP is not detected, and that is not a hole to
be closed by tightening the bound: one ULP is the smallest difference two
doubles can have, so a tolerance excluding it would be a tolerance of zero,
and a correct reimplementation in a language with wider intermediates or FMA
contraction would then fail against correct code.

## 2. Unit-normalisation tolerance (C4-IV-04): analytic bound 2 ULP

**Its own row, not inherited from the round-trip figure.** Section (b) above
states why: the round-trip bound is derived taking the folded constant as
given; this one is derived over the conversion that PRODUCES it, entering the
same physical quantity in a different unit.

### The derivation

`VOLUME_TO_UL.uL` and `CONCENTRATION_TO_UG_PER_ML['ug/mL']` are both exactly
1. Multiplying by 1 is exact in IEEE 754: a quantity entered in the BASE unit
carries no conversion rounding at all. A quantity entered in any other unit
(mL, mg/mL) carries exactly one conversion multiply, at most half an ULP.

Worst case: staining volume entered in mL AND stock concentration entered in
mg/mL together, compared against both entered in their base units.

| Path | Operations | Relative error from the true value |
|---|---|---|
| Base units (µL, µg/mL) | 0 conversion multiplies, 1 division to form `volumePerConcentration` | <= 0.5 ULP |
| Non-base units (mL, mg/mL) | 2 conversion multiplies, 1 division | <= 1.5 ULP |

The two paths are compared against EACH OTHER, not against a round trip on a
shared constant, so their errors do not cancel: the bound on their difference
is the SUM, **0.5 + 1.5 = 2 ULP**.

**Measured**: 50,000 swept cases at the worst-case unit pairing (volume in mL
against volume in µL, concentration in mg/mL against concentration in µg/mL,
together), over the same eleven-decade, non-round input distribution as
section (c) above. Worst observed **1 ULP**, zero cases exceeding. Recorded in
`src/lib/invariance.test.ts`, `describe('C4-IV-04, the unit paths agree over
the path including normalisation')`.

## 3. Ratio-test tolerance: analytic bound 6 ULP, compared with "less than or equal"

C4-IV-03: the ratio between the unrounded concentrations of consecutive points
equals the declared dilution factor at every point, including the last.

### THE BUILD'S EARLIER 4 ULP FIGURE IS WITHDRAWN

It was the maximum observed over 3.3 million cases, not a derived bound, and
C4-CN-01 as amended forbids treating a sample maximum as a tolerance: a case
the sweep did not happen to draw is not thereby proven impossible. The
distribution is kept below, as evidence for the bound that replaces it, not as
the bound.

### The derivation

`integerPower` (`src/lib/generate.ts`) builds `f^(i-1)` by exponentiation by
squaring. Counting only the multiplies that actually ROUND matters here:
`square *= square` always rounds, but `result *= square` is EXACT whenever
`result` is still 1, which holds up to and including the exponent's first set
bit. An instrumented count of every exponent from 0 to 11 (verified against
the algorithm's own trace, not asserted from it) gives:

| Exponent | Rounding multiplies |
|---|---|
| 0 | 0 |
| 1 | 0 |
| 2 | 1 |
| 3 | 2 |
| 4 | 2 |
| 5 | 3 |
| 6 | 3 |
| 7 | 4 |
| 8 | 3 |
| 9 | 4 |
| 10 | **4** |
| 11 | **5** |

Summing consecutive pairs (exponent i, i+1) gives the worst combined count at
**(10, 11)**, the LAST ratio of a 12-point series, matching the reasoning that
already names the last point in C4-IV-03 as the place an accumulating error is
largest:

```
f^10:  4 rounding multiplies
f^11:  5 rounding multiplies
point[10] = top / f^10:        1 division
point[11] = top / f^11:        1 division
ratio = point[10] / point[11]: 1 division
                                -----------------------------
                                9 multiplies + 3 divisions = 12 operations
```

Each operation contributes at most half an ULP, so the analytic bound is
**12 x 0.5 = 6 ULP**. This is a requirement on how the generation method is
structured, not an observation about one run of it: an implementation that
generated the series another way, including by repeated multiplication or by
`Math.pow`, could exceed it, which is precisely why C4-IV-06 makes the method
something the tool must state.

**Measured**, over 3,300,000 consecutive ratios: twelve points, dilution
factors drawn from 1.01 to 20, top points across ten decades. Distribution of
the error, rounded up to whole ULPs:

| Error | Count |
|---|---|
| 0 ULP | 2,028,739 |
| 1 ULP | 1,200,613 |
| 2 ULP | 69,139 |
| 3 ULP | 1,500 |
| 4 ULP | 9 |
| above 4 ULP | 0 |

The empirical maximum, 4 ULP, sits two ULP under the 6 ULP analytic bound:
evidence the bound is not loose, kept beside the bound rather than in place of
it.

### The choice of primitive is inside the tolerance, not beside it

`Math.pow`'s disagreement with the stated method is ENGINE-DEPENDENT, since
ECMAScript does not require it to be correctly rounded, and its magnitude is
therefore reported rather than asserted to a fixed figure
(`src/lib/ratio.test.ts`): non-zero and of the same order as the tolerance on
every engine this has been run on, between 2 and 4 ULP across engines observed
during development. At a factor of 1.7 the two primitives disagree; at a
factor of 3, where every power up to the eleventh is an exact integer, they
agree exactly, which is why the non-integer case is in the fixture set rather
than an afterthought, a confirmation run only at integer factors would have
found no difference and concluded there was none to find.

## 4. Ratio-test sensitivity, per C4-IV-05

MEASURED, pending the item 6 record, signed with it rather than separately.
Generated by repeated multiplication with a rounding to a stated number of
significant figures inserted at each step, which is how a spreadsheet does it
and therefore the defect worth confirming against.

| Inserted rounding | Worst ratio error | Detected against a 6 ULP bound |
|---|---|---|
| 17 significant figures | 1 ULP | No |
| **16 significant figures** | **3 ULP** | **No. The coarsest undetected** |
| **15 significant figures** | **35 ULP** | **Yes. The detected precision** |
| 13 significant figures | 3.1e3 ULP | Yes |
| 10 significant figures | 3.0e6 ULP | Yes |
| 6 significant figures | 2.6e10 ULP | Yes |

**Stated on the tool's own page, in plain terms, per C4-IV-05:** a defect
smaller than approximately one rounding per step is invisible to the ratio
test. A repeated-multiply implementation that rounded no more coarsely than 16
significant figures would be indistinguishable from the stated method by this
test alone; sixteen figures is close enough to the seventeen a double carries
that the defect is genuinely almost absent, but a reader of acceptance 8 is
entitled to know what the confirmation does not cover.

## 5. Reimplementation agreement: 1 ULP, observed 0

Not named in open item 6, derived here because acceptance 3 needs a comparison
basis.

**Measured**: exact agreement, 0 ULP, over more than 200 compared values
across eleven fixtures, between the TypeScript engine and an independent
Python implementation written from the URS.

The bound is nonetheless **1 ULP and not bit-identical**, for the reason C1
gives for its own: making bit-identical the requirement would generalise one
measured pair of implementations into a claim about every future one, and a
language with wider intermediates can differ in the last bit while being
entirely correct. The observed figure is asserted separately, so any drift
away from exact agreement is visible rather than absorbed by the bound.

Consequence, stated: a defect uniformly smaller than one ULP is invisible to
acceptance 3 and to acceptance 5 alike.
