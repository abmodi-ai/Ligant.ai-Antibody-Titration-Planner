Open item 6 asks for the round-trip and ratio-test tolerances to be derived over
the stated generation method, and derived **separately**: section 11 says the
ratio-test tolerance "does not inherit the round-trip figure".

Both are measured here rather than chosen, and both are stated as REQUIREMENTS
rather than as observations. The distinction matters and C1 records it for its
own bound: an observation describes what one implementation happened to do,
while a requirement constrains what any implementation may do, and only the
second is worth putting in a specification.

## The stated generation method, per C4-IV-06

Each point is computed from the top point and its integer index as
`top / f^(i-1)`, with `f^(i-1)` formed by **exponentiation by squaring** and
applied as a **single division**. `Math.pow` is not used. Points are not
generated by repeated multiplication from the preceding point.

Both tolerances below are derived over this method and no other. A change to it
invalidates the derivation, not merely the numbers.

## 1. Round-trip tolerance: 1 ULP, compared with "less than or equal"

C4-IV-01: a concentration converted to a volume per test and back, at the same
staining volume and stock concentration, returns the input.

**Measured**: 200,000 cases, concentrations across eleven decades, stock
concentrations across eight, staining volumes across five. Worst observed error
**1.0 ULP**, zero cases exceeding.

### This is a requirement on how the conversion is structured

The round trip is two operations, a multiply and a divide by the SAME folded
constant, because `normalise.ts` folds the staining volume and the stock
concentration into one number at entry. Each operation contributes at most half
an ULP, so the analytic bound is 1 ULP and the measurement meets it exactly.

Applied stepwise the trip would be six operations and the bound three times as
loose. C1 measured precisely that on its own two-factor conversion and found the
stepwise path reaching 3.0 ULP and exceeding a 1 ULP bound in 0.54 per cent of
cases, against zero exceedances folded, and additionally found the stepwise
intermediate underflowing where the folded divisor did not.

**A tool inheriting this row as an observation would fail the bound.** The
figure is not transferable without the structure that produces it.

## 2. Ratio-test tolerance: 4 ULP, compared with "less than or equal"

C4-IV-03: the ratio between the unrounded concentrations of consecutive points
equals the declared dilution factor at every point, including the last.

**Measured**: 3,300,000 consecutive ratios. Twelve points, dilution factors
drawn from 1.01 to 20, top points across ten decades. Distribution of the error,
rounded up to whole ULPs:

| Error | Count |
|---|---|
| 0 ULP | 2,028,739 |
| 1 ULP | 1,200,613 |
| 2 ULP | 69,139 |
| 3 ULP | 1,500 |
| 4 ULP | 9 |
| above 4 ULP | 0 |

### Why it does not inherit the round-trip figure

A consecutive ratio involves two divisions by two separately computed integer
powers, and each power is itself a short chain of multiplications under
exponentiation by squaring. That is a longer error path than the folded round
trip, and the measurement shows it: four times the bound, with a tail that
decays sharply rather than a hard edge.

### The choice of primitive is inside the tolerance, not beside it

Measured at a factor of 1.7, whose integer powers are not exactly
representable, a `Math.pow`-generated series differs from a
squaring-generated one by up to **4 ULP**, which is the whole of the derived
bound. A series generated with `Math.pow` could therefore sit at the edge of the
tolerance before any real defect had been introduced.

At a factor of 3 the two agree exactly, because every power up to the eleventh
is an integer below 2^53. **A confirmation run only at integer factors would
have found no difference and concluded there was none to find**, which is why
the non-integer case is in the fixture set rather than an afterthought.

## 3. Sensitivity, bounded on both sides

R18 requires the ratio confirmation to record the precision at which detection
occurs AND the coarsest precision at which it does not. The same discipline is
applied to the round trip, because the same question applies and the answer is
not the comfortable one.

### The round trip, under an inserted displacement

| Inserted | Measured | Detected against a 1 ULP bound |
|---|---|---|
| 1 ULP | exactly 1.0 ULP | **No.** It sits precisely on the bound |
| 2 ULP | exactly 2.0 ULP | Yes, on all six points |
| a clamp at 4 µL | above 1e12 ULP | Yes, on the 1 point above the ceiling |
| a floor at 2 µL | above 1e12 ULP | Yes, on the 4 points below the floor |

A displacement of exactly one ULP is **not detected**. That is not a hole to be
closed by tightening the bound: one ULP is the smallest difference two doubles
can have, so a tolerance excluding it would be a tolerance of zero, and a
correct reimplementation in a language with wider intermediates or with FMA
contraction would then fail against correct code.

The clamp and the floor are RANGE defects and reach only the points on their own
side of the threshold. The counts are stated exactly rather than as "at least
one", so that a future change moving a point across either threshold shows up.

### The ratio test, under an inserted rounding

Generated by repeated multiplication with a rounding to a stated number of
significant figures inserted at each step, which is how a spreadsheet does it
and therefore the defect worth confirming against.

| Inserted rounding | Worst ratio error | Detected against a 4 ULP bound |
|---|---|---|
| 17 significant figures | 1 ULP | No |
| **16 significant figures** | **3 ULP** | **No. The coarsest undetected** |
| **15 significant figures** | **35 ULP** | **Yes. The detected precision** |
| 13 significant figures | 3.1e3 ULP | Yes |
| 10 significant figures | 3.0e6 ULP | Yes |
| 6 significant figures | 2.6e10 ULP | Yes |

**Stated rather than left implicit:** a repeated-multiply implementation that
rounded no more coarsely than 16 significant figures would be indistinguishable
from the stated method by this test alone. Sixteen figures is close enough to
the seventeen a double carries that the defect is genuinely almost absent, but a
reader of acceptance 8 is entitled to know what the confirmation does not cover.

## 4. Reimplementation agreement: 1 ULP, observed 0

Not named in open item 6, derived here because acceptance 3 needs a comparison
basis.

**Measured**: exact agreement, 0 ULP, over more than 200 compared values across
eleven fixtures, between the TypeScript engine and an independent Python
implementation written from the URS.

The bound is nonetheless **1 ULP and not bit-identical**, for the reason C1
gives for its own: making bit-identical the requirement would generalise one
measured pair of implementations into a claim about every future one, and a
language with wider intermediates can differ in the last bit while being
entirely correct. The observed figure is asserted separately, so any drift away
from exact agreement is visible rather than absorbed by the bound.

Consequence, stated: a defect uniformly smaller than one ULP is invisible to
acceptance 3 and to acceptance 5 alike.
